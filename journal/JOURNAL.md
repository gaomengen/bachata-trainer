## 2026-10-07 · Diagonal lead into the box step

**Source:** class

**What I learned**
For the box step, instead of starting with a plain side basic to the left, the leader
can step **diagonally forward-left** on count 1 and finish the box from there. Two
ways to use it:
1. **Diagonal entry:** diagonal on the first step only, then the regular box. Once
   the follow has read it, there's no need to keep signalling.
2. **All diagonal:** keep the whole box on the diagonals, including going diagonally
   back.

**Why it works**
- With a plain side basic, counts 1–4 of a box feel the same as any side basic, so the
  follow only finds out it's a box on count 5.
- The diagonal step moves the leader's frame toward the follow. They feel themselves
  sent diagonally back on count 1, so they know a box is coming straight away.
- The lead comes from the body (chest and frame), not the arms. If the arms push, it
  feels like a shove.
- Bonus: cutting the corner makes the box smaller, which helps on a crowded floor.

**Try next**
- Practise the diagonal entry with different partners and notice whether they pick
  up the box on count 1.
- Check that the push comes from my frame, not my arms.
