# Bachata Knowledge: Master Notes

## Leading & following
- **Lead from the body, not the arms.** Moving the frame and chest gives a clear signal;
  pushing with the arms feels like a shove. *(2026-10-07)*
- **Signal early.** The follow needs to know what's coming on count 1, not count 5.
  A change of direction in the leader's first step is a clear, early signal.
  *(2026-10-07)*
- **The leader is responsible for the follow's safety.** You invited them, so: no
  grabbing or yanking, and never lead them into other dancers. Watch the floor behind
  your follow, since they're the one moving backward. *(2026-10-13)*
- **Trust is built through the connection.** The eyes-closed drill shows the follow
  can dance from the connection alone when the lead is clear. *(2026-10-13)*

- **Leading a new dancer:** read what they can do and give them patterns that feel
  good. *(2026-10-22)*
- **Commit to the lead.** A confident, simple lead feels better to a follow than a
  hesitant fancy one; mixed signals leave them guessing. Going back to the basic is a
  decision, not a failure. *(2026-10-18)*

- **Lead even in choreography.** The follow may know what's next, but prep and signal
  anyway; that keeps you together and builds the social habit. *(2026-10-20)*
- **Three connection points: middle two fingers, elbow, shoulder.** Connected, the arm
  carries the signal from the body; one loose point swallows it. *(2026-10-20)*
- **Hands move with the body.** The frame is torso plus hands as one unit; hands left
  behind make even the basic unclear. *(2026-10-20)*
- **Strong is fine, a shove isn't.** Follows want a clear, firm signal: from the body,
  through a connected frame, smooth. Never sudden or yanked from the arm; scale it to
  the partner. *(2026-10-20)*
- **Check tension out loud.** "Do you feel me?" Show the pull with the first knuckles
  of the middle two fingers, from the hand, not the arm. *(2026-10-20)*

## Body & technique
- **Soft knees, quiet upper body.** Bent knees absorb each step so you don't stomp and
  your height stays level; the upper body stays tall and quiet (it travels with the
  step but doesn't lean or twist). *(2026-10-07, refined 2026-10-08)*
- **"Your weight" = your hip.** Your centre of mass sits low in the pelvis, so weight
  transfer means moving the pelvis from over one foot to over the other.
  *(2026-10-08)*
- **Foot, then torso, then hip.** The foot lands lightly on the ball of the foot with
  no weight; the torso starts moving (that's the lead); the hip arrives last and
  settles over the foot (that's the transfer). *(2026-10-07, refined 2026-10-08)*
- **The hip delay** is the gap between torso and hip, and it's what makes the hip
  movement visible. The torso moves upright, as a unit; never lean. *(2026-10-08)*
- **The delay gives drive.** While the hip is still over the standing leg, that leg is
  loaded and can push you into the step. Hip-first means nothing to push with.
  *(2026-10-08)*
- **Torso-first keeps you upright.** Leading with the hip leaves the upper body behind
  and bends you into a "C"; leading with torso and foot ends with shoulders, hip and
  foot stacked. *(2026-10-08)*
- **Fully commit the weight transfer.** The weight goes completely onto one foot, never
  split. Split weight forces an extra adjustment before the next move, which makes
  you late. *(2026-10-20)*
- **The hip comes from the knees.** When the weighted leg straightens a little and the
  other softens, the hip tilts by itself. Don't push it. *(2026-10-07)*
- **Head level and over the body**, not leading forward. *(2026-10-07)*

## Steps & patterns
### The tap (count 4 and 8)
- **Most common beginner mistake: missing the tap.** It carries no weight, so the body
  doesn't feel it and rushes into the next 1. *(2026-10-08)*
- **The opposite mistake: weighting the tap.** The tap is a touch; the tapping foot
  has to stay free because it moves next. *(2026-10-08)*
- **Fixes:** give the tap a job (the hip settles and pops), say "tap" out loud
  instead of "4", count "4 &" to hold the gap. *(2026-10-08)*

### Box step
- **Diagonal entry:** step diagonally forward-left on 1, then finish the regular box.
  The follow reads it straight away. *(2026-10-07)*
- **All diagonal:** the whole box turned 45°, including the diagonal back.
  *(2026-10-07)*
- The diagonal makes the box smaller, which helps on a crowded floor. *(2026-10-07)*

### Patterns & sides
- **Many partner patterns are done on one side only.** The hold is asymmetric, so a
  mirror is really a different pattern; social dancing relies on everyone knowing the
  standard version. *(2026-10-11)*
- **Both sides:** the basics, right and left turns, and solo turns/styling (to keep
  my body balanced). *(2026-10-11)*
- Learn patterns as taught first; mirror later for variety. *(2026-10-11)*

## Practice & learning
- **There's no fast track.** Understanding helps, but only repetition builds it into
  the body. *(2026-10-08)*
- **Rewatch class clips** again and again until I feel confident. *(2026-10-08)*
- **Learning choreography:** one 8-count at a time, drill the joins, dance it without
  the video, slow then up to tempo, film and compare. Natural comes after
  memorised. *(2026-10-08)*

- **A home practice plan takes the dread out of socials.** Knowing what I'm working on
  turns a social from a test into practice. *(2026-10-12)*
- **Film a baseline, then compare.** Progress is easier to see on video than to feel.
  *(2026-10-12)*

- **Class is recognition; a social is recall.** Under pressure you only have what's
  automatic. Drill until a pattern needs no recording, like vocabulary in a language:
  you don't talk with a dictionary. *(2026-10-14)*
- **Free dancing = recombining absorbed moves.** Muscle-memorise each move until it's
  part of me, like the basic turns. Test: can I do it while my attention is on the
  music, the floor or my partner? *(2026-10-21)*
- **Chunking:** as the vocabulary grows, new patterns are recognised as known pieces in
  a new order, so learning speeds up. *(2026-10-22)*
- **Learned isn't absorbed.** A move can work in practice and still vanish on the
  floor; remembering under pressure is the last stage. A quick rewatch between dances
  helps in the meantime. *(2026-10-22)*
- **Flip un-mirrored videos.** Copying a face-to-face instructor puts you on the
  opposite foot. Flip the video horizontally, slow it to 0.5×, loop one 8-count at a
  time. *(2026-10-22)*
- **Keep a vocabulary list:** every pattern named, with a one-line cue; practise from
  the name, not the video. *(2026-10-14)*
- **Full-song shadow dancing** with an imaginary partner, returning to the basic
  between patterns. Moves first, then practise the lead as part of each move.
  *(2026-10-14)*
- **Solid basics speed up learning:** when the basics are automatic, attention is free
  for new patterns. *(2026-10-14)*

## Musicality
- **The "and" is the half-beat between numbers:** 1 & 2 & 3 & 4. Counting it
  (subdivision) helps me feel the time between steps, prepare on the "&", and land
  on the number without rushing. *(2026-10-07)*
- **The güira plays the "and"**: the "ch-ch-ch" of the metal scraper fills the
  half-beats. *(2026-10-07)*
- **Syncopation = a step on an "and" that's normally empty.** Classic bachata version:
  "3 & 4" replaces the tap with two quick steps. *(2026-10-07)*
- Counting builds muscle memory; later the count fades and you just hear the music.
  *(2026-10-07)*
- **Texture:** varying the steps instead of hitting every count evenly: step weight
  ("dun dun toe"), double steps, holds, slides, accents. *(2026-10-10)*
- **Texture follows musical accents.** Syncopation is the most common trigger; also
  breaks and stops (hold), the mambo section (bigger, busier), and strong hits on the
  beat. *(2026-10-10)*
- **Bachata comes in phrases of eight counts, usually four eights at a time.** Fills
  and builds tend to come at the end of a phrase, so changes can be predicted even
  in an unfamiliar song. *(2026-10-10)*
- **My "syncopation reserve" (vocabulary):** drilled textures ready to use when the
  music offers a moment. Drill them until automatic; save them for the moments, since
  texture works by contrast with the plain basic. *(2026-10-10)*

## Bachata styles
- **Dominican / traditional:** footwork-heavy, grounded, playful, lots of solo
  expression. Music: requinto guitar, bongo, güira, bass; derecho/majao/mambo
  structure. The style I enjoy most. *(2026-10-15)*
- **Sensual:** developed in Spain; body waves, turns, dips, head movement, close
  connection. Music: often slower remixes of pop/R&B songs, with pop structure
  (verse, chorus, breaks, drops). What the LA socials dance. *(2026-10-15)*
- **Match practice to where I dance.** Footwork builds grounding and texture but
  doesn't replace partner patterns at a sensual social. *(2026-10-15)*
- On pop remixes, listen for breaks, drops and builds rather than majao/mambo.
  *(2026-10-15)*
- **Shine = a short sequence of solo footwork** (term from salsa: partners let go and
  each "shines"). *(2026-10-22)*
- **Shines at socials:** at Dominican nights, all the time (often in open hold, both
  partners doing footwork). At sensual socials, in small doses: 4–8 counts on a
  break or an energetic section, then reconnect; or pieces of a shine as texture
  inside the basic. Keep it short, invite the partner in, always come back to the
  connection; keep it very short with a beginner. *(2026-10-22)*

## Social dancing & floor craft
- A smaller box (from the diagonal entry) takes up less room on a busy floor.
  *(2026-10-07)*
- **Find the beat with the basic first.** Dance 8–16 counts of basic until you and your
  partner are together on the music, then add patterns. Lost mid-song? Go back to the
  basic and find the 1. *(2026-10-14)*
- **When your head is busy choosing moves, timing goes first.** *(2026-10-14)*
- **If a partner breaks off mid-dance,** stay graceful: don't make a scene, invite
  them back, finish the song. *(2026-10-18)*
- **Choosing partners:** go by how people actually behave, not first impressions.
  Early in the night, ask people who look like they're enjoying themselves.
  *(2026-10-18)*
- Some LA classes have 6–8 leads per follow; ask early and make each dance count.
  *(2026-10-14)*

## Teaching ideas
- **Eyes-closed trust drill.** Pairs in a circle; the follow's hands on the lead's chest,
  eyes closed; the lead follows the instructor's signals: forward, back, hold.
  Variations: eyes open first, add side steps and the basic, add music, swap roles,
  ask follows for feedback. *(2026-10-13)*
