From class to social: why my patterns disappeared

I could do the moves in class. At the social I could remember four. What happened, and the plan that fixed it.

From my bachata learning journal · October 14, 2026

I spent a month in Los Angeles taking as many classes as I could. One Wednesday was a perfect example of the gap between class and social dancing.

The class was a win

I was picking up patterns faster than ever. My basics (step, tap, where to put my feet) had finally become solid, so my attention was free for the new material. I could even see the instructor's next move coming.

The social was a defeat

I danced one song, with one partner. We weren't on the beat for the whole song. All I could remember was a left turn, a right turn, a cross body lead and a box step. That was it. I tried the move I'd practised from the last class and it didn't click.

Partway through, my partner let go and started dancing on her own. I tried not to make it awkward and invited her back in. She said, "Why not try the thing we learned today?" It didn't feel good.

Looking back, I can see her side. The dance was off the beat the whole time, so she was probably frustrated with the dance, not with me. Her comment may even have been a clumsy attempt to help, since we'd both just come out of the same class. Etiquette is to finish the song, but I'm glad I stayed calm and invited her back.

What actually went wrong

Class is recognition. A social is recall. In class, the pattern is right in front of you, you repeat it over and over, and you know what's next. At a social you have to choose a move, lead it, keep the time and read a stranger, all at once. Under that load, your brain only gives you what's truly automatic. For me that was four moves.

The timing problem had the same cause. When my head was busy choosing moves, the beat was the first thing to go. We probably never found it together in the first place.

The plan

Learning to dance is a lot like learning a language. You don't talk to people with a dictionary open. You talk because the words are already in you. So:

  1. Drill until I don't need the recording. I rewatch my class videos over and over, until I can do each move without them.
  2. Keep a vocabulary list. Every pattern, with its name and a one-line cue. I practise from the name, not the video. If I can't do it from the name, it goes back on the pile.
  3. Rehearse full songs. Any bachata song, an imaginary partner, and the moves I know, coming back to the basic between patterns, the way you do at a social.
  4. Find the beat first. At the social, dance the first 8 to 16 counts as a plain basic until we're clearly together on the music. If I get lost, go back to the basic and find the 1.

A week later

I went to the next social with one goal: use the move I'd been memorising. I danced three songs. On the first one I tried the move and couldn't pull it off. So I went to the bathroom, rewatched the video, came back, and did it with my next partner. Later I also got in a hammerlock and a Madrid walk.

That taught me one more thing: learned isn't the same as absorbed. A move can work fine at home and still vanish on the floor. Remembering it under pressure is the last stage. I've started using a simple test: can I do the move while my attention is somewhere else, on the music or on my partner? If yes, it's ready.

That's the path to free dancing, as far as I can tell. Free dancing isn't making up new moves on the spot. It's putting together moves you no longer have to think about.
