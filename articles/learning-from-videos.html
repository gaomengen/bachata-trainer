Learning footwork from videos: flip it first

Why copying a face-to-face video is so slow, how to fix it in ten seconds, and what to do with a shine once you've learned it.

From my bachata learning journal · October 22, 2026

I found a bachata shine on TikTok with amazing footwork and great music, and decided to learn it. It went really slowly. I kept using the foot I don't normally lead with, and every pattern felt like I was learning it twice.

Why it's slow

The instructor was facing the camera. When they step with their left foot, it shows up on the right side of your screen. If you copy it like a mirror, you step with your right. So you end up learning the whole thing on the side the instructor isn't using, which is usually your weaker side too.

The fix: flip the video

Flip it horizontally. Then their left foot appears on your left, and you copy it with the same foot they use.

  • iPhone: save the video, then Photos → Edit → the crop tool → the flip button.
  • CapCut (free): import the clip and tap Mirror. You can slow it down there too.

I downloaded the video, mirrored it, and it was so much easier to learn.

How I learn a shine now

  1. Slow it down to half speed.
  2. Learn one 8-count until I can do it from memory, then add the next.
  3. Count out loud, "and"s included. Shines are full of syncopation.
  4. Film myself and compare with the flipped video.
  5. Once it's absorbed, learn the mirrored side too. It's solo work, so both sides keeps you balanced.

What's a shine, anyway?

A shine is a short run of fancy solo footwork. The word comes from salsa, where partners let go and each "shines" on their own for a moment. There's no fixed length. A single footwork idea is usually 4 or 8 counts, and a full combo strings several together, anywhere from 16 to 64 counts. Either way it's built in 8-counts, because the music is phrased in 8s.

Can you use it at a social?

  • At a Dominican or traditional night, all the time. Footwork is the heart of the style, and partners often stay in open hold while both play with their feet.
  • At a sensual social, in small doses. Let go for 4 to 8 counts on a break or during the mambo, then reconnect. Or use a piece of a shine as texture inside your basic while you're still holding your partner.

Keep it short, invite your partner in with a look and a smile, and always come back to the connection. With a beginner partner, keep it very short so they don't feel left out.

There's no fast track

Every one of these steps comes down to the same thing: repetition. Watch it, dance it without the video, film it, compare, repeat. It isn't glamorous, but it's the only thing I've found that works.
