Count the "and"

Saying "and" between the numbers made my steps easier overnight. It also turned out to be the door into syncopation.

From my bachata learning journal · October 7, 2026

Most people count bachata as 1, 2, 3, 4. Some teachers count it differently: "1 and 2, 3, 4," or "1, 2, 3 and 4." The first time I tried counting the "and," my steps just got easier. I didn't have to think about them. The next move came naturally.

It took me a while to understand why. There are really two different things going on.

1. The "and" as the space between beats

The "and" is the half-beat between two numbers: 1 & 2 & 3 & 4. Counting it on every beat doesn't add any steps. It fills in the gaps, so your body can feel the time between the numbers. Musicians call this subdivision.

Why it helps:

  • The "and" is where you get ready. Your weight shifts, your knee softens, your foot starts to move. Then the step lands right on the number.
  • It stops you rushing. Beginners (me included) tend to hit the next step early, especially after the tap. Counting the "and" holds you in the gap.
  • It's in the music. That in-between pulse is mostly the güira, the metal scraper going "ch-ch-ch." When you count the "and," you're tuning in to it.

2. A step on the "and"

Syncopation is something else: you actually put a step on an "and" that's normally empty.

The classic one in bachata replaces the tap with two quick steps: "3 & 4". Instead of step, tap, you go step, step, step. So when a teacher counts "1, 2, 3 and 4," that's usually what they're setting up.

How they fit together

Counting the "and" everywhere is the timing tool. Stepping on one particular "and" is the move. Get comfortable with the first and the second comes almost for free, because the gap is already in your body.

Try it

  • Count "1 & 2 & 3 & 4" out loud through a whole song with just the basic. No extra steps.
  • Then try "3 & 4" in place of the tap a few times.
  • Last, stop counting and listen for the güira. You'll hear where the "and" is.

You don't have to understand all of this before you start. Just count it and move. The counting builds the muscle memory, and later the count fades and you simply hear the music.
