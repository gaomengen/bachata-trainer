Don't skip the tap

The most common beginner mistake is losing a count, and it's almost always the tap. Here's why it disappears and how to keep it.

From my bachata learning journal · October 8, 2026

If there's one mistake I see beginners make more than any other (and I've made it plenty), it's missing a count. And it's almost always the same one: the tap.

Why the tap disappears

It doesn't carry any weight. On 1, 2 and 3 your weight moves, so your body feels them as steps. The tap on 4 doesn't move your weight, so your body doesn't really register it as a count. You skip straight to the next 1. Your 4-count turns into a 3-count, and within a few bars you're off the music.

The rush. After three steps your body wants to keep walking. Waiting through a beat where nothing moves feels strange at first.

The opposite mistake

Some beginners do take the tap, but they step down on it. Then the wrong foot is free for the next count, and the next basic starts on the wrong side. The tap is a touch, not a step. The tapping foot has to stay free, because it's the one that moves next.

How to keep it

  • Give the tap a job. The tap is where the hip settles and pops. If you let your hip finish its movement on 4, the beat feels full instead of empty. (More on that in What is your weight?)
  • Say it out loud. Count "1, 2, 3, tap" instead of "4" for a while. The step animations on Steps & Styles count it that way.
  • Use the "and". Count "3, 4 &, 1." The "and" after the tap holds you in place, so you don't jump into the next 1 early. (See Count the "and".)

It sounds small. But the tap is the signature of bachata. It's the difference between dancing and walking to music.
